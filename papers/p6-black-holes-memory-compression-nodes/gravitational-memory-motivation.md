# Gravitational Memory Motivation — Black Holes, Dark Matter, and Collapse History

## Purpose

This note preserves a bounded research motivation for the P6 black-hole memory-compression track and the broader SoCT/PNT gravity-as-memory interpretation.

It records an interpretive bridge between three mainstream-adjacent facts or research areas:

1. black-hole information preservation / evaporation questions;
2. dark matter as the dominant gravitational scaffold of the cosmic web;
3. cosmic-web structure as an accumulated collapse-history pattern, with dense filaments and underdense voids.

This note is not evidence that SoCT or PNT is confirmed. It is a motivation note for why the gravity-as-memory hypothesis is worth formalizing and testing.

## Mainstream-safe premise

The mainstream-safe framing is:

- black holes create an information problem because quantum theory expects information preservation while classical evaporation appeared to threaten information loss;
- modern black-hole physics generally expects information is preserved or encoded in some way, though the exact mechanism remains debated;
- dark matter is inferred through gravity and dominates the matter budget associated with large-scale structure;
- dark matter is organized into halos, filaments, and clusters, while cosmic voids are underdense rather than absolutely empty;
- the cosmic web is history-dependent: gravitational collapse accumulates structure over time.

## SoCT/PNT-compatible interpretation

SoCT/PNT may interpret these facts through a memory-field lens:

```text
collapse history
  -> compressed gravitational / horizon / field state
  -> later radiative, dynamical, or structural expression
```

Black holes then become natural candidates for memory-compression nodes:

```text
black-hole intake history
  -> horizon / gravitational / interior compression state
  -> evaporation residue, information-preserving correlations, or parent-child transfer
```

Cosmic structure becomes a large-scale memory-density map:

```text
voids      -> low collapse history / low memory density / dark-matter underdensity
filaments  -> high collapse history / accumulated memory density / strong dark-sector scaffolding
halos      -> localized collapse-history wells around galaxies and clusters
```

This supports the research intuition:

> Gravity may be not only a response to current visible matter, but also a record of accumulated collapse history.

P6 frames black holes as memory-compression nodes. This note adds a nearby motivation:
