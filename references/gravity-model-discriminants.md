# Gravity-Model Discriminants — CDM, MOND-Like Models, Emergent Gravity, and SoCT

**Status:** Research-comparison scaffold  
**Purpose:** Define empirical observables that can distinguish standard dark-matter interpretations, modified-gravity alternatives, emergent-gravity proposals, and the current Sequence of Collapse Theory (SoCT) gravitational-memory hypothesis.

## Scope

This document is not a ranking of theories and does not treat galaxy anomalies as evidence for SoCT by default. It identifies where the current SoCT/P1 program must differ observationally from existing explanations if it is to become a scientific gravity model rather than a reinterpretation layered over known phenomenology.

The current P1 working hypothesis is history dependent:

```text
G_eff(x,t) = G0 [1 + alpha M(x,t)]
M(t) ~ 1 - exp(-t/tau)
```

with the preliminary empirical question framed as whether formation-history / age proxies correlate with inferred outer mass discrepancy after controlling for ordinary covariates.

## Claim Boundary

- Rotation-curve anomalies do not by themselves validate modified gravity or SoCT.
- A baryon-acceleration correlation does not uniquely select MOND.
- A successful halo fit does not by itself prove particle dark matter.
- A galaxy-age correlation does not establish a memory field unless standard assembly-history, stellar-population, environment, and selection explanations are excluded.
- SoCT must derive quantitatively distinct predictions rather than relabeling residuals after the fact.

## What SoCT Must Explain Beyond Existing Galaxy Phenomenology

A scientifically useful SoCT gravity program cannot stop at "older galaxies have more inferred dark matter." At minimum it must address four competing explanations:

1. **Halo assembly history:** older systems may occupy systematically different halos.
2. **Stellar-population systematics:** age, color, and mass-to-light estimates are coupled.
3. **Baryonic structure:** surface density and radial acceleration already organize much of rotation-curve phenomenology.
4. **Environment:** tides, satellite history, local density, and cosmic-web location can alter inferred dynamics.

SoCT becomes more distinctive only if a history-dependent residual survives these controls in a form predicted before the final fit.

## P1 / P2 Discriminating Tests

### Test A — Age residual at fixed baryonic acceleration

**Question:** Does stellar-population age or a validated formation-history proxy explain residual rotation-curve discrepancy after conditioning on baryonic acceleration, surface brightness, stellar mass, gas fraction, morphology, and radius?

**SoCT expectation:** A positive history-dependent residual remains if accumulated memory contributes independently.

**Conventional alternative:** The age association disappears once baryonic structure and halo/assembly proxies are included.

**Falsification / constraint:** No stable age coefficient across robust age measurements, subsets, and multivariate models.

### Test B — Inner versus outer history dependence

**Question:** Is the age/history signal systematically stronger in outer regions than inner regions under the same sample definition?

**SoCT expectation:** A structured radial contrast may appear where baryonic closure is weakest or where accumulated-history effects are predicted to dominate.

**Alternative explanations:** Radial stellar-population gradients, gas distribution, beam/sampling effects, and halo concentration.

**Falsification / constraint:** No reproducible inner/outer differential beyond conventional structural models.

### Test C — Matched galaxies with different history

Construct pairs or matched samples similar in:

- baryonic mass;
- surface brightness;
- gas fraction;
- morphology;
- characteristic acceleration;
- environment;
- radius coverage;

but different in independently measured formation history.

A genuine SoCT history effect should predict a residual difference not reducible to present-state baryonic observables.

### Test D — Environment cross-match

Cross-match P1 galaxies with environment metrics or cosmic-web classifications.

**Purpose:** Determine whether the age/fDM relation survives, strengthens, reverses, or disappears after environment is modeled.

**Important boundary:** Environment dependence is not uniquely SoCT because Lambda-CDM assembly bias and MOND external-field effects can also produce environment-linked signatures.

### Test E — High-redshift analogue

At fixed or carefully modeled baryonic structure, compare systems at different cosmic ages.

**SoCT expectation:** If memory accumulates with history, young high-redshift disks should not require the same effective memory contribution as mature low-redshift systems.

**Required caution:** Selection, gas fraction, turbulence, size evolution, stellar-population inference, and resolution effects are severe confounds.

## Model-Comparison Ladder

Use increasingly demanding models rather than comparing SoCT only with a naive Newtonian baryons-only curve.

### Baseline 0 — Baryons only

Useful as a diagnostic, but scientifically insufficient as the sole comparator.

### Baseline 1 — Empirical baryonic-acceleration relation

Test whether SoCT adds predictive value beyond the observed radial-acceleration relation.

### Baseline 2 — MOND-like acceleration law

Compare residuals using a fixed, preregistered MOND interpolation choice rather than tuning per galaxy.

### Baseline 3 — Standard halo model

Use an NFW-like or other explicitly selected halo prescription with transparent priors and baryonic mass-to-light assumptions.

### Baseline 4 — History-aware conventional model

Add assembly/history proxies, concentration proxies, environment, morphology, and stellar-population variables.

### Candidate SoCT model

Only after the baselines above should a memory term be evaluated, ideally with a low parameter count and an out-of-sample prediction target.

## Strong Evidence Standard for SoCT Gravity

A persuasive SoCT result would require more than a lower in-sample residual. A stronger standard is:

1. a preregistered history-dependent observable;
2. independent formation-history measurements;
3. survival after baryonic, environment, and assembly controls;
4. radial behavior predicted before analysis;
5. improved out-of-sample prediction versus strong conventional baselines;
6. a parameterization that transfers across datasets;
7. a derived lensing implication; and
8. consistency with cosmological constraints.

Without these, the result should remain "consistent with" or "motivating," not evidence for a new gravitational law.

## Reference-Use Boundary

These sources define strong neighboring baselines. Their successes or failures do not validate SoCT. Their role is to raise the standard SoCT must meet: a history-dependent memory model must predict something that current baryonic relations, halo assembly, MOND-like dynamics, and emergent-gravity models do not already explain.
